Introducing

The Design Year

The Design Year is an intimate, guided program for high-achieving mothers navigating one of the most disorienting transitions of their professional lives.

You built a career. Then you had a child. Now you're figuring out who you are in both.

You're at the peak of your career. Leadership role. Strong compensation. A great partnership. And you decide — it's time to start a family.

Nobody told you that the version of you who walks back into that office after maternity leave would feel like a stranger in her own life. That the confidence you built over a decade could feel so fragile. That you'd sit in a meeting where you used to lead the room and find yourself wondering if you still belong there.

Nobody talks about the grief of leaving behind a version of yourself you worked so hard to build. Or the guilt of wanting your career back when you love your child more than anything. Or the loneliness of being surrounded by high-achievers and not knowing a single one who admits to feeling exactly this.

That's who this is for.

Returning to work after having a child isn't just a scheduling problem. It's a neurological shift, an identity reconfiguration, and a relationship renegotiation — all happening at the same time, while you're also trying to perform at a high level professionally.

Most support out there treats it like a logistics problem. The Design Year treats it like what it actually is.

Who this is for

This program is for you if you're pregnant, newly postpartum, or in the thick of early childhood — and you're a woman who has never stopped being ambitious, even when everything feels impossible to hold at once.

You're returning to work or already back — and something still feels off.

You love your child fiercely and also miss the version of yourself that felt purposeful.

You're carrying more than anyone around you fully understands.

You want tools, not platitudes. Community, not content. And someone who has actually done the thing you're trying to do.

If you nodded at any of this — you belong here.

Program Partner

Rachel Kurasz

This cohort is supported by Rachel Kurasz, PhD candidate at Northern Illinois University, where she researches how people communicate across difference, rebuild civil dialogue, and develop critical thinking skills in a world that makes all three harder than it should be.

In early motherhood, many women lose the language to articulate what they're experiencing — to their partners, their employers, their communities, and themselves. Rachel's research gives us a framework for that. Because before you can redesign your life, you have to be able to speak it.

She's also a mother to a 7-year-old daughter — which means she brings lived experience alongside the academic rigor. This isn't theory applied to motherhood. It's motherhood informing the theory.
